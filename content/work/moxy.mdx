## Problem

The pandemic broke the way we work. The Great Resignation pushed millions back into the interview chair, and the chair had not been redesigned in twenty years.

I had access to a self-selecting research panel: the BrainStation students I had been informally coaching. Talking to them surfaced one pain that was almost universal, almost untouched by existing products, and almost addressable with off-the-shelf tools. Interviews.

The data backed up the conversations. **39% of candidates get rejected because they do not appear confident at interview**. The average employer spends **over $4,000 per hire**. Both sides want the same thing. Neither side enjoys the process.

Existing prep tools were fragmented. A flashcard app here, a coaching service there, a YouTube channel somewhere else. Nothing put study, practice and feedback in one place.

## Hypothesis

<Hypothesis

The hard part was never the questions. The hard part was making practice feel less like an audit and more like rehearsal.

## Process

I interviewed users from Canada to Qatar to validate the problem. Dovetail caught the pattern: candidates were drowning in advice and starving for reps.

I ran an ideation workshop with my design meetup and a separate brainstorm with classmates. Four epics fell out: study common questions, get tips on structure, record yourself practicing, store recordings for review.

The first prototype was a UI mistake. I leaned into neumorphism because it looked cool and I wanted my visual chops to keep up with my research chops. Guerrilla testing in a London pub validated the concept and gutted the UI in the same evening.

<Flowchart

Three rounds of testing later, the homepage and social feed got cut. The functionality narrowed. The component library got built. The third test was clean.

Brand work followed. The name came from a word-association round with users. "Skills" became "moxie", trimmed to "Moxy" for the wordmark. The palette landed on blue and green for security and growth, with a yellow accent for joy and a red functional highlight for record states. Every combination passes AAA WCAG.

Moxy was a coursework project. No live metrics, by design.

What the project gave me was a workflow. I now know what I am like as a solo UX lead: research-loud, ideation-prolific, fast at prototyping, slow to be precious about my own work. I also know where my edges are. Secondary research creep almost killed the project in the first week. I now ringfence it.

## Learnings

- **The interesting question is the second one.** "Why are interviews hard?" is generic. "What is the loop that would make practicing them not feel like homework?" is the one that produced the product.
- **Test before you draw the fancy UI.** Neumorphism cost me a week. Validating the loop on paper would have given me the same insight for nothing.
- **Brand attributes ladder up.** Once "calm, fluid, courageous" was the brief, every typeface, color and curve had a yes/no test. Most decisions stopped being decisions.
