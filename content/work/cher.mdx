## Problem

The brief was a sealed envelope. The teams were assembled days in advance. I had four people: two designers, two engineers. I had 24 hours from envelope to demo.

So I prepped the only thing I could prep. The team.

I ran two alignment sessions before the hackathon. We mapped capabilities and assumptions. We set up a shared Notion workspace and built a Figma-native ticketing system so designer-to-developer handoff would not eat the clock on the day.

Envelope opened. Topic: ride sharing.

Secondary research turned up the live wire. **Almost half of all transport pollution comes from people driving alone.** Cars built for five carry one. Ride sharing changes the math, but only if people will actually share. Existing players had solved strangers-in-cars for taxi rides. Nobody had solved it for peer ride sharing where the driver and passengers are both civilians.

## Process

User interviews in a Hoxton hotel bar. Yes, the hotel bar. Two designers, one Marvel POP prototype, and several Londoners patient enough to sit through us.

The findings clustered three ways. Small talk with strangers is awkward. Online trust signals work but only when they are specific. And, most acutely, **1 in 3 women said they would not feel comfortable sharing a car with a man, especially after a night out**.

Persona work in this kind of timeframe is a stick. We used it to keep us on track when caffeine started losing.

Ideation: competitor research, burgers, Crazy 8s. We sketched everything from wireless proximity matching to wearables with light messaging, then cut down to what we could ship: a mobile app with profiles, search and messaging.

Solution sketching stayed deliberately clean. A minimal UI cut development time and gave the engineers reusable components from the first hour. They started coding while the designers refined the brand.

The brand came out of the work. **Cher** is "share" with the phonetics tilted, "a Car for HER", and a wink at the pop icon. The logo fuses two ancient Greek Venus symbols into a smiling face that doubles as a route between two points. The colour palette tested against WCAG.

Final hours: pitch deck, narrative arc, presentation rehearsal. Handoff was clean because we had set up the ticket pipeline two days earlier.

We pitched. We did not win the hackathon. We were, however, the only team that ran primary research before sketching. The investment offer came from a person at the next table in the hotel bar who had watched us test for an hour and asked who we were.

## Learnings

- **Prepare what you can prepare.** The envelope was sealed but the team was not. Two alignment sessions before the hackathon bought us six hours on the day.
- **Constraints make the brand.** I would never have arrived at "Cher" without the time pressure. The associative jump from "share" to "for her" to "Cher" was a 90-second whiteboard riff.
- **Primary research compounds.** Every team in the room did secondary research. We were the only team that talked to users. That single decision was worth a whole production day.
