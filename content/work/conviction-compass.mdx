## Problem

Founders kept telling us they had product-market fit. Their dashboards told a different story.
The gap between what teams believed about their market and what the data could actually support
was the most expensive thing in their company — and nobody had a tool that exposed it without
feeling like an audit.

We talked to thirty-two operators before a single line of code was written. The pattern was
consistent: they had strong convictions about user behavior, but those convictions were almost
always shaped by their three loudest customers and a handful of recent wins.

## Hypothesis

<Hypothesis
  belief="Founders will self-administer a structured diagnostic of their own beliefs"
  reason="they already journal assumptions privately, but the writing is unstructured and rarely re-read"
  signal="40% of teams complete the diagnostic without prompting within seven days of signup"
/>

The hard part wasn't the questions. It was the framing. A diagnostic that *feels* like an audit
gets abandoned at question four. A diagnostic that *feels* like a journal gets finished and
shared with the team.

## Process

<Canvas
  src="/work/conviction-compass/canvas.svg"
  caption="The hypothesis canvas — three columns: what we believe, why, and what would change our mind."
  alt="Hypothesis canvas with three columns"
/>

We started with paper. Twelve operators ran through a printed version of the canvas in
forty-five-minute sessions. Three things came out of that:

- The "what would change our mind" column was the hardest to fill in and the most valuable
- Operators wanted to **share** their canvas with one or two trusted people, not publish it
- The diagnostic only worked if it captured the *date* the belief was recorded — beliefs without
  timestamps couldn't be falsified later

<Flowchart
  src="/work/conviction-compass/flowchart.svg"
  caption="The week-by-week falsification loop. Beliefs become tests, tests become evidence, evidence updates the canvas."
  alt="Falsification loop diagram"
/>

The product followed the paper. We built the canvas, the share primitive, and the timestamp
infrastructure first. Everything else was scaffolding.

## Outcome

<Stat value="4.2×" label="activation lift, week 6" />
<Stat value="30" label="seed-stage teams in beta" />
<Stat value="71%" label="completed the diagnostic without prompting" />

The signal we predicted — 40% completion without prompting — was wrong on the low side. Once
the journal framing was in place, operators completed it because they wanted the artifact for
themselves, not because we asked them to.

## Learnings

- **Frame before form.** The diagnostic existed on paper for three weeks before any UI. That
  saved us at least a month of building the wrong thing.
- **The hardest column is the most valuable.** "What would change our mind" was the column
  operators wanted to skip and the column that produced every useful conversation in user
  interviews.
- **Falsifiability needs timestamps.** A belief without a date can't be wrong. We almost shipped
  without timestamping the canvas entries.
